✦ Project Overview



This activity focused on developing a personal visual identity through the use of a logo, color palette, typography, tagline, and personal information layout.

For this project, I created a minimalist brand system based on my initials, C and K. The final outputs show how the same visual identity can be applied to a primary logo, a tagline presentation, a color-and-typography guide, and a personal profile card.

Rather than using many colors or decorative graphics, I concentrated on strong letterforms, neutral colors, clear alignment, and generous spacing. This approach allowed the design to feel modern, recognizable, and consistent across all four compositions.

💭 The Idea Behind the Design

My personal brand is centered on simplicity, identity, creativity, and confidence.

I wanted the design to feel:

Minimalist

Modern

Professional

Creative

Confident

Personal

The main logo combines my initials into a stylized “CK” monogram. Joining the two letters creates a single symbol that can be recognized without displaying my full name. The flowing curve of the C contrasts with the taller and more angular structure of the K, giving the mark both movement and stability.

The phrase “Since 2004” connects the visual identity to my personal story and birth year. It makes the logo feel established and gives the brand a clear point of origin.

I also used the tagline:

“Life in Cotton”

The phrase suggests comfort, simplicity, authenticity, and an everyday lifestyle. It supports the clean visual direction of the brand because cotton is often associated with something natural, familiar, and versatile.

The identity is further described through the words:

DATA • DESIGN • CREATIVITY

These words represent my interest in organizing information, developing visual ideas, and producing creative work. Together, they give the personal brand a clearer purpose beyond the logo itself.

🎨 Color System



A restrained neutral palette was selected to make the visual identity timeless, versatile, and easy to apply across different materials.

Dark Gray (#222222): Primary logo tone and a softer alternative to pure black.

Black (#000000): Strong emphasis, maximum contrast, and bold information.

Warm Off-White (#EEEDE9): Supporting background, visual comfort, and negative space.

Dark Gray — #222222

Dark gray serves as the main tone of the identity. It gives the monogram a strong presence while appearing slightly softer and more refined than pure black.

The color supports the minimalist direction of the project and communicates stability, maturity, and professionalism. It can be used for the logo, headings, and other primary visual elements.

Black — #000000

Black provides the strongest contrast in the visual system. It is used to emphasize important information such as the name, tagline labels, contact details, and selected headings.

Its bold appearance adds confidence and clarity to the compositions. Because the overall palette is limited, black also helps maintain consistency across every output.

Warm Off-White — #EEEDE9

The warm off-white background provides a clean visual surface without looking as harsh as pure white. It gives the dark elements enough space to stand out while adding a subtle sense of warmth.

This background color is important because it creates negative space, readability, and a calm visual atmosphere throughout the brand system.

🔤 Typography System

Typography is one of the most important parts of this personal branding project. I selected a combination of expressive display lettering and functional sans-serif typefaces so the identity could appear creative while remaining readable.

Sego

Sego is the most decorative typeface in the typography set. Its unusual curves and stylized letterforms give the brand an editorial and fashion-inspired quality.

This typeface supports the distinctive appearance of the monogram and can be used for selected display elements where visual personality is more important than long-form readability.

Be Vietnam

Be Vietnam provides a clean and modern appearance. Its lighter and simpler letterforms contrast with the more decorative display font.

It is suitable for supporting phrases, labels, and short text because it keeps the design clear without competing with the logo.

Archivo Black

Archivo Black is a heavy display typeface used to create emphasis. Its thick letterforms give names, labels, and important details a confident visual weight.

It works effectively for elements such as “Since 2004,” my name, and other information that needs to be noticed immediately.

DM Sans

DM Sans is a simple and highly readable supporting typeface. It balances the heavier and more decorative fonts by providing a neutral option for smaller information.

It can be used for contact details, captions, descriptions, and other content that requires clarity.

Why These Fonts?

The typography choices were made to create a balance between personality and function.

The decorative typeface makes the identity more recognizable, while the sans-serif fonts keep the supporting information readable. Archivo Black provides strong emphasis, whereas Be Vietnam and DM Sans create lighter visual levels.

Using different font weights and styles also establishes hierarchy. However, each typeface has a specific role, preventing the typography system from appearing random or inconsistent.

🧩 Visual Elements

CK Monogram

The CK monogram is the central element of the project. It combines the initials of Carlson Kerk into one continuous visual mark.

The rounded form of the C creates movement, while the tall vertical stroke and diagonal leg of the K provide structure. This contrast helps make the logo simple but memorable.

“Since 2004” Marker

The phrase “Since 2004” gives the identity a personal origin. Placing it directly below the monogram connects it closely with the logo and makes the design resemble an established brand mark.

Tagline



The tagline “Life in Cotton” adds meaning and character to the logo. It expands the identity from a simple set of initials into a lifestyle-oriented concept associated with comfort and simplicity.

The tagline is centered below the primary mark so that it supports the logo without taking attention away from it.

Personal Identity Words

The words Data • Design • Creativity summarize the interests represented by the brand. The centered dots act as separators and keep the three ideas visually connected.

This line uses lighter typography than the name, creating a clear distinction between the owner of the identity and the qualities associated with it.

Contact and Location Icons

The personal information layout uses recognizable icons for email, telephone, and location. These symbols allow viewers to identify each type of information quickly.

The icons and bold text demonstrate how the personal branding system can be extended into a contact card or professional profile while maintaining the same visual style.

Negative Space

Large areas of open space are intentionally maintained around the logo and information. This keeps the design from becoming crowded and directs attention toward the most important elements.

The use of negative space also strengthens the minimalist character of the brand.

🖼️ Final Compositions

The project contains four coordinated outputs that demonstrate different uses of the identity system.

1. Primary Logo

The first composition presents the CK monogram with “Since 2004.” Its centered placement and open background allow the logo to become the main focal point.

2. Logo and Tagline

The second composition adds “Life in Cotton” beneath the primary logo. This version communicates more of the brand's concept while preserving the same centered hierarchy.

3. Color and Typography Guide

The third composition documents the selected color palette and typography set. It acts as a simple style guide that can help keep future designs consistent.

4. Personal Information Layout



The fourth composition applies the identity to a personal profile layout containing the name, identity words, contact categories, and location. It demonstrates how the brand can work in a practical communication material.

Together, the four outputs form a small but complete identity system rather than four unrelated designs.

Design Principles I Applied

1. Visual Hierarchy

The large monogram is positioned as the primary focal point. The name, tagline, identity words, and contact details appear at smaller levels, helping the audience understand the information in order of importance.

2. Contrast

Dark gray and black elements create clear contrast against the warm off-white background. Different font weights also separate bold information from supporting text.

3. Alignment

Centered alignment creates order in the logo and tagline compositions. In the profile card, the information is arranged across the page while maintaining a clear visual structure.

4. Balance

The centered logo compositions use symmetrical balance, while the profile layout distributes information across the left, center, and right areas. This keeps the composition stable despite the large amount of open space.

5. Repetition and Consistency

The same colors, fonts, logo, spacing style, and bold text treatments are repeated throughout all four outputs. This repetition makes the designs recognizable as parts of one identity.

6. Simplicity

The designs use only essential elements. The absence of unnecessary decoration allows the monogram, typography, and information to communicate clearly.

How I Developed the Final Outputs

I began by using my initials as the foundation of the personal brand. I experimented with combining the forms of C and K until they could function as one unified monogram.

After developing the primary mark, I added “Since 2004” to connect the identity with my personal history. I then created a version featuring the “Life in Cotton” tagline to provide the brand with a clearer theme.

Next, I selected a neutral palette and assigned specific roles to the dark gray, black, and warm off-white colors. I also developed a typography set that included both decorative and highly readable fonts.

Finally, I applied the visual system to a personal information layout. I reviewed the spacing, alignment, hierarchy, and contrast of every composition to ensure that the outputs remained consistent.

What I Learned

This activity taught me that personal branding involves more than creating a logo. A complete identity requires consistent colors, purposeful typography, recognizable visual elements, and clear rules for arranging information.

I learned how a monogram can represent a person's identity using only a few letterforms. I also discovered that a limited neutral palette can still create a strong impression when contrast, scale, and spacing are used effectively.

Most importantly, I learned that typography should be selected according to function. Decorative fonts can build personality, while simple fonts are necessary for readability. When these elements work together consistently, they create a more professional and memorable identity.
