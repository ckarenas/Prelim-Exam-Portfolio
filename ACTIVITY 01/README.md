Activity 01: Presentation Design Principles

Overview

The first activity focused on applying presentation and visual design principles to create clear and engaging promotional material.

For this activity, I designed a poster announcing a free coffee booth at Arrupe Hall on Tuesday, August 4, 2026. Its main promotional message is:

“Free coffee. No catch. Just caffeine and good vibes.”

The purpose of the poster is to capture the attention of students, communicate the offer immediately, and present the important event details in an organized way. I combined typography, illustrations, color, spacing, and decorative patterns to give the poster a friendly campus atmosphere.

Instead of using a realistic coffee-shop style, I chose a limited blue-and-cream color palette and playful line illustrations. This allowed the design to appear lively and creative while still connecting visually with the identity of Ateneo de Davao University and the School of Nursing.

My Design Concept

My concept combines coffee, student energy, and Ateneo identity.

The coffee theme is shown through several hand-drawn illustrations, including cups, rising steam, and hands holding drinks. These elements make the promotion understandable even before the viewer reads all the text. The illustrations also create a casual and welcoming mood that fits the message of sharing coffee and good vibes with students.

I used deep blue and warm cream as the main colors. The blue reflects the visual identity associated with Ateneo, while the cream background adds warmth and prevents the design from feeling too dark or formal. Using only two dominant colors also helps the different sections look connected.

The checkerboard patterns at the top and bottom add movement and personality to the poster. They give the design a slightly retro, café-inspired appearance while framing the information inside the page. The School of Nursing seal further identifies the group connected with the activity.

Overall, I wanted the poster to feel energetic, student-friendly, and visually memorable while keeping the event information readable.

Design Principles I Applied

1. Visual Hierarchy

Visual hierarchy was created by changing the size, weight, style, and placement of the text.

The large handwritten words “FREE COFFEE” quickly introduce the main offer. The dark-blue information panel on the right then attracts attention because of its strong color and large size. Inside this panel, the main message is presented in bold capital letters, followed by the availability reminder and the Arrupe Hall location.

The date is placed in the upper-left area using a large serif font, while the School of Nursing seal and a coffee illustration appear beside it. This arrangement guides the viewer from the date and school identity toward the promotion and location.

Through this hierarchy, viewers can understand the most important parts of the poster even when looking at it for only a few seconds.

2. Contrast

The design uses strong contrast between the deep-blue elements and the light-cream background.

The cream lettering inside the blue panel is easy to notice and read because the two colors are clearly separated. In the other areas, blue text and illustrations stand out against the plain background.

Contrast is also created through typography. The elegant serif font used for the date, the loose handwritten font used for “FREE COFFEE,” and the bold block fonts inside the panel each have a different appearance. These differences help distinguish the types of information without introducing additional colors.

3. Alignment

Alignment helped divide the poster into clear visual sections.

The date and handwritten promotional phrase are mainly aligned on the left side. The large rounded information panel occupies the right side, while the seal and smaller cup illustration are positioned near the upper-right area.

Inside the blue panel, the quotation, availability reminder, and location are arranged vertically. This keeps related details within one organized space and makes the reading direction easy to follow.

Although the composition is not perfectly symmetrical, the alignment gives each element a clear position and prevents the layout from appearing random.

4. Asymmetrical Balance

I used asymmetrical balance instead of placing identical elements on both sides of the page.

The tall blue panel creates strong visual weight on the right side. This is balanced by the large handwritten text and group of illustrated coffee cups on the left. The date in the upper-left is balanced by the School of Nursing seal and cup illustration in the upper-right.

The checkerboard decorations at the top and bottom also help anchor the composition. Even though the two sides contain different elements, their size and placement make the overall design feel stable.

5. Emphasis

The main emphasis is placed on the offer of free coffee.

The phrase appears twice: first as large handwritten lettering and again as part of the bold quotation inside the blue panel. Repeating the offer makes the purpose of the poster immediately clear.

The blue panel also emphasizes the event details by separating them from the rest of the composition. The large block lettering used for the Arrupe Hall information helps viewers identify the location after reading the main promotion.

6. Repetition and Consistency

Repetition and consistency are present throughout the poster to create a unified appearance.

The same blue-and-cream colors are repeated in the text, borders, illustrations, and information panel. Coffee cups and hand gestures appear in different areas, reinforcing the theme without relying only on written information.

The checkerboard design is repeated along the upper and lower edges. Rounded and curved shapes are also used in the coffee illustrations and the large information panel. These repeated visual details connect the sections and make them appear part of one design.

7. Proximity

Proximity was used to show which details belong together.

The main quotation, the phrase “First come, first served,” and the Arrupe Hall information are grouped inside the same blue panel. This indicates that they are all part of the same promotion.

The date, School of Nursing seal, and small coffee illustration are placed near the top because they introduce when the activity will happen and who is connected with it. The illustrated coffee cups are grouped at the bottom-left, allowing them to function as one supporting visual rather than several unrelated images.

Grouping these elements makes the poster easier to scan and understand.

8. Simplicity and Negative Space

Although the poster includes several illustrations and text styles, the overall design remains simple because it uses a limited color palette and clearly separated sections.

The open cream-colored spaces prevent the layout from becoming too crowded. These empty areas give the major elements room to stand out and help the viewer move naturally from one section to another.

The poster focuses only on the necessary information: the free coffee offer, date, availability, location, and school identity. This keeps the message direct and understandable.

How I Developed the Final Output

I began by identifying the most important information that the poster needed to communicate: the free coffee promotion, event date, location, and first-come-first-served reminder.

Next, I selected a blue-and-cream palette to connect the design with Ateneo while maintaining a warm and inviting appearance. I divided the portrait layout into two main visual areas: an open section on the left and a strong information panel on the right.

I placed the date and handwritten “FREE COFFEE” text on the left, then organized the main quotation and event details inside the blue panel. After establishing the information hierarchy, I added coffee-themed line illustrations to strengthen the concept and make the poster more engaging.

The checkerboard borders were added as decorative framing elements. Finally, I reviewed the spacing, alignment, color contrast, and relative size of each element to make sure the poster remained balanced and readable.

How the Design Principles Improved My Poster

Applying design principles helped transform the poster from a simple collection of text and graphics into an organized promotional material.

Visual hierarchy and emphasis made the free coffee offer and event information easy to identify. Contrast improved readability through the strong difference between blue and cream. Alignment and proximity grouped connected details and provided a clear reading flow.

Asymmetrical balance allowed the layout to feel creative without becoming unstable. Repetition and consistency connected the coffee illustrations, school colors, and checkerboard patterns. Typography gave each type of information a distinct role, while simplicity and negative space kept the composition from feeling overcrowded.

Together, these principles made the poster more readable, visually coordinated, and appropriate for a student-centered promotional event.

What I Learned

This activity taught me that an effective poster must communicate quickly while still creating a strong visual impression.

I learned that a limited color palette can produce a unified design when contrast is used properly. I also learned that different typography styles can establish personality and hierarchy, but they must be arranged carefully to avoid confusion.

The activity also helped me understand how asymmetrical balance, repetition, proximity, and negative space can organize a composition. Most importantly, I realized that every visual element—including colors, patterns, illustrations, and font choices—should support the purpose of the message.
